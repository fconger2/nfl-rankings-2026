What goes into the score

Each stat is weighted by how consistent it's been this season: how well a QB's odd-week number matches his even-week number. Consistent stats reflect skill; noisy ones get less say.

Method

  • Data: nflverse play-by-play for the 2026 regular season only.
  • To be ranked, a QB must have taken at least —% of his team's snaps (measured as his share of the team's dropbacks).
  • A QB's plays are every dropback (passes, sacks, scrambles) plus his designed runs. Kneels, spikes and two-point tries are left out.
  • Opponent adjustment: each play's EPA has the defense's average EPA allowed (relative to league average) subtracted, separately for dropbacks and runs.
  • Score: each stat becomes a z-score against the qualified QBs, the weighted z-scores are summed, and the total is rescaled so 50 is average and 10 is one standard deviation.
  • Offense efficiency: EPA per play on every pass and run his team ran in the games he played, so a QB on a strong unit gets credit for it. "Run game EPA/play" is the non-QB runs, shown for context only.
  • Turnover rate gets almost no weight because interceptions and fumbles have barely carried over from one half of the season to the other.
  • Projections: per-game passing and rushing rates are blended with four games of league-average QB production, then multiplied by the team's remaining games. The current starter is whoever had the most dropbacks in his team's latest game.
